# proof of purchase

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

A business can ask for proof of purchase when a consumer requests a repair, replacement or refund, but the law does not define how much proof is enough; the consumer only needs to reasonably demonstrate they bought the item. Suppliers must provide a proof of transaction on request for purchases under $75 within 7 days, and automatically for purchases of $75 excluding GST or more, though the ACCC states the automatic threshold as over $75 while Consumer Affairs Victoria states it as $75 excluding GST or more. Clear photos, photocopies, electronic copies and receipt or reference numbers can be valid proof, but a supplier or manufacturer does not have to accept a claim if the consumer cannot show where the product was purchased, though it may still choose to do so. Retailer practice varies: Kmart as of October 2025 accepts Kmart branded products without proof only by providing an exchange voucher, while Officeworks and BIG W require proof and Target has refused photocopied receipts, a practice CHOICE describes as contrary to the Australian Consumer Law.

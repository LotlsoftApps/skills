# change of mind

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

A store generally does not have to give a refund or replacement when a customer simply changes their mind; under the Australian Consumer Law, a customer is only entitled to a refund or replacement for a major problem with a product covered by consumer guarantees. If a store has a change-of-mind policy, it must follow it, and that policy cannot take away those rights; a store may lawfully refuse change-of-mind refunds on sale items, but a blanket "no refunds or exchanges on sale items" policy is illegal as of 21 July 2026. Policies vary: Myer offers change-of-mind refunds or exchanges on most items within 30 days but excludes clearance items and suspected reseller or bulk-purchaser sales, while Kmart requires change-of-mind returns within 60 days, or 365 days for OnePass members, in resaleable original packaging, and does not refund delivery fees. Separate rules apply to telemarketing, door-to-door and public-place sales, where the consumer has 10 business days to cancel for any reason without charge, or 6 months if the salesperson does not tell them about the cooling-off period.

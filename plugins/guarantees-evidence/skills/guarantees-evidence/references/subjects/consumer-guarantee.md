# consumer guarantee

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

Consumer guarantees are automatic and cannot be taken away; warranties are extra promises a business can choose to make, cannot replace, change or take away a consumer's basic rights, and the consumer guarantee of acceptable quality usually still applies after a warranty against defects expires. Businesses must not tell a consumer that a consumer guarantee does not exist, may be excluded, or may not have a particular effect, and must not mislead consumers into thinking their rights are limited to a warranty's remedies or timeframe; the mandatory warranty-against-defects information must be available with the actual product, not only on a website or in-store. A contract term is void to the extent it purports to exclude, restrict or modify the consumer guarantees, and the ACCC's guide records a July 2013 Federal Court order that Hewlett-Packard pay a $3 million civil pecuniary penalty for false or misleading representations to consumers and retailers regarding consumer guarantee rights. Consumer Affairs Victoria states that a person or business is a consumer for products or services costing up to $100,000, with a $40,000 threshold for purchases before 1 July 2021; products or services bought before 1 January 2011 are not covered by the current Australian Consumer Law, and the unfair contract terms protections also apply to small business contracts entered into or renewed after 12 November 2016.

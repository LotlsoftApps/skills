# product recall

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

Product recalls are common: in 2021 almost 400 were issued, and ACCC data suggests only about half of affected products are returned to sellers. A responsible Minister may issue a recall notice by written notice published on the internet, and a business may need to recall a product and notify the Australian Government Minister within 2 days if the product is a safety risk or does not comply with a mandatory standard or ban; under s128 a supplier must tell the ACCC within 2 days of taking a recall action. A recall does not automatically amount to a major failure under the consumer guarantees; each recalled good is considered individually, the standard repair, replace or refund provisions apply, and which remedy a consumer gets depends on the risk analysis of the recall. CHOICE reports that there is no recall standard and no benchmarks that a recall process must meet, and suppliers usually ask consumers voluntarily to dispose of or return a recalled product for a refund, replacement or modification; Kmart states it will accept a product return under consumer guarantees where the product has been recalled.

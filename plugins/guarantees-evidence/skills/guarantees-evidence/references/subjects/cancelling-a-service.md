# cancelling a service

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

For services covered by the current Australian Consumer Law, a consumer cannot cancel merely because they changed their mind, because the problem is outside the supplier’s control, because they want the service provided against the supplier’s advice, or because they did not clearly communicate what they wanted. Consumer Affairs Victoria states that cancellation takes effect when the consumer tells the supplier they intend to cancel, verbally, in writing or by other reasonable means. If a consumer is entitled to cancel a services contract, Consumer Affairs Victoria states they have a right to a refund, and if the contract includes products they are also rejecting those products and can recover the money paid for them; but from 1 July 2026 the Australian Consumer Law limits recovery for services to the extent the services have not already been consumed at termination. Where a business makes a material change to a service, consumers are likely entitled to the remedies for major problems with a service; CHOICE states the Australian Consumer Law does not cover complaints about telecommunications, and services bought before 1 January 2011 are not covered by the current law.

### Documented position (10)

- **[documented-position]** The Australian Consumer Law section 269(3) entitles a consumer who terminates a services contract to recover a refund of money paid only to the extent that the consumer has not already consumed the services at the time the termination takes effect.
  - as of: 2026-07-01
  - quote: "(3) The consumer is entitled to recover, by action against the supplier of the services, a refund of:"
  - quote: "(a) any money paid by the consumer for the services; and"
  - quote: "to the extent that the consumer has not already consumed the services at the time the termination takes effect."
  - source: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00109/2026-07-01/2026-07-01/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_4/document_4.html

# no refunds sign

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

Consumers cannot agree to give up their rights to the consumer guarantees, and a "no refunds" sign is unlawful if it implies that no refund is possible in any circumstance, including where goods or services are faulty. A sign saying "No refunds will be given if you have simply changed your mind" is acceptable, and businesses may display a sign at the point of sale, including online, alerting consumers to their rights under the consumer guarantees, though displaying one is not compulsory. Penalty figures in the cited sources conflict: as of 13 October 2023 Consumer Affairs Victoria states a maximum penalty of $500,000 for a person while the ACCC guide states $220,000 for an individual, and as of 1 July 2026 the Australian Consumer Law compilation states a fine of not more than $2,500,000 for a person other than a body corporate. A business giving a consumer a repair notice cannot satisfy that requirement by showing the consumer a sign or directing them to information on a website.

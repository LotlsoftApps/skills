# consequential loss

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

Consequential loss is the cost to a consumer of a problem with goods or services, usually financial but capable of including lost time or productivity, and it is the reasonably foreseeable loss in time and money from the failure. A consumer can apply directly to the business for damages or compensation, and can go to formal dispute resolution or legal action if the business refuses; this compensation is in addition to any repair, replacement or refund, and manufacturers are also liable for loss or damage from safety defects causing death or injury or damage to personal property. Under the consumer guarantees, the ACCC and Consumer Affairs Victoria state that a business cannot write a term into a sales contract saying it will not be responsible for this extra loss, and such terms may be misleading or unfair contract terms. A business does not have to pay for damage or loss that relates to something independent of the business after a product left its control, the loss must not be caused by something outside human control such as a natural disaster, and section 64A of the Australian Consumer Law permits a term limiting liability for failure to comply with a guarantee for goods not ordinarily acquired for personal, domestic or household use or consumption, unless the buyer establishes that it is not fair or reasonable for the supplier to rely on the term.

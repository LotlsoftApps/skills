# manufacturer warranty

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

A manufacturer warranty is a voluntary extra promise that becomes a legal right once the consumer buys the product or service; it does not limit or replace the automatic consumer guarantees, and after it expires the consumer guarantee of acceptable quality usually still applies. A consumer can make a claim under consumer law regardless of any warranty and may be entitled to a repair, replacement or refund depending on whether the problem is major or cannot be fixed; a store generally cannot insist the consumer deal directly with the manufacturer, even if the claim falls under a manufacturer's warranty. A warranty against defects must be in writing and must include a statement that rights under the warranty sit alongside the Australian Consumer Law guarantees, which cannot be excluded; specific mandatory warranty text applies to services and to goods and services supplied after 9 June 2019. In CHOICE's 2023 mystery shop, more than seven in ten stores misrepresented a consumer's rights to a remedy after the warranty period, and courts and the ACCC have treated such statements as false or misleading; the exact duration of Australian Consumer Law rights is uncertain.

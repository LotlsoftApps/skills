# spare parts

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

Products and services bought before 1 January 2011 are not covered by the current Australian Consumer Law. Under that law, a manufacturer or importer must take reasonable action to ensure repair facilities and spare parts are reasonably available for a reasonable period after supply, except where the supply is by sale by auction; if the only problem is that spare parts or repair facilities are not available a reasonable time after purchase, the seller may direct the consumer to the manufacturer instead. A consumer can recover costs from the manufacturer for a failure to comply with this guarantee, including an amount for reduction in the product's value and in some cases compensation for damages or loss; manufacturers and their local authorised sellers do not have to provide spare parts under warranties for parallel imports if the warranty terms exclude them. What counts as a reasonable time is not fixed: Consumer Affairs Victoria says it may be reasonable to expect tyres for a new car to be available for a number of years, while it may not be reasonable to expect spare parts for an inexpensive children's toy to be available at all, and the Productivity Commission found limited case law on the spare part guarantee but concluded the current drafting of section 58 is not a significant barrier, while also finding significant and unnecessary barriers to repair for some products.

# store credit

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

For a major problem, a consumer can choose a refund, and the seller must not refuse that option or insist on a credit note, exchange card or replacement. Signs such as 'exchange or credit note only for return of sale items' are unlawful, 'we will only exchange, repair or give credit notes' is generally against the law, and false or misleading information about consumer guarantees or remedies carries a maximum civil penalty of $1.1 million for a body corporate and $220,000 for an individual. A business with a change-of-mind policy offering a refund, replacement or credit note must comply with that policy, and refunds should be in the same form as the original payment unless the business and consumer agree otherwise. The claims settle those points but do not state whether a seller can insist on store credit for a minor problem; they state only that the seller can offer a refund for a minor problem.

# durability

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

The Australian Consumer Law came into force on 1 January 2011 and replaced previous Commonwealth, state and territory consumer protection legislation. Under it, acceptable quality includes durability: goods must be as durable as a reasonable consumer would regard as acceptable, there are no set rules for how long any product should last, and the assessment depends on factors such as price, type, complexity, frequency of use, and representations. Consumer guarantees apply for a reasonable time, apply whether or not an extended warranty is purchased, and cannot be modified or removed by an express or manufacturer warranty, and a reasonable consumer can expect a good to last longer than the supplier or manufacturer's warranty period depending on the circumstances. The specific positions conflict: Kogan said it would refund faulty products over $1,000 only within the first six months while CHOICE said expensive items should last longer than six months and consumers are entitled to a refund if they do not; JB Hi-Fi staff said responsibility ended after two years, while Consumer Affairs Victoria said a $6,000 TV reasonably includes more than two years' use and may entitle the buyer to a repair, replacement or refund from the store.

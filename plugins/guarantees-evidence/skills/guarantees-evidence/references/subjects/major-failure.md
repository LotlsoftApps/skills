# major failure

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

Products and services bought before 1 January 2011 are not covered by the current Australian Consumer Law. Under that law, a major problem is one where a reasonable consumer would not have bought the product if they had known of the problem beforehand. For a major failure, the ACCC states that the business must give the consumer the choice of remedy, and Australian Consumer Law section 259(3) allows the consumer to recover compensation for any reduction in the goods' value below the price paid. In practice, this is not always honoured: in 2022, 71% of 80 Harvey Norman, JB Hi-Fi and The Good Guys stores misrepresented consumer rights, and 18 of 26 Harvey Norman stores CHOICE called did so, most commonly by saying free remedies after the warranty period were slim or nonexistent; a 2023 class action also alleged JB Hi-Fi extended warranties were of little or no value because customers already had the same rights for free under the Australian Consumer Law.

### Documented position (5)

- **[documented-position]** Australian Consumer Law section 259(3) provides that where the failure cannot be remedied or is a major failure, the consumer may recover compensation for any reduction in the value of the goods below the price paid.
  - as of: 2026-07-01
  - quote: "(3) If the failure to comply with the guarantee cannot be remedied or is a major failure, the consumer may:"
  - quote: "(b) by action against the supplier, recover compensation for any reduction in the value of the goods below the price paid or payable by the consumer for the goods."
  - source: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00109/2026-07-01/2026-07-01/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_4/document_4.html
- **[documented-position]** The ACCC states that when a business sells a product with a major problem, the business must give the consumer the choice of a remedy.
  - as of: 2026-07-21
  - quote: "When a business sells a product with a major problem, or a product that later develops a major problem, it must give the consumer the choice of a:"
  - source: https://www.accc.gov.au/business/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-sold/repair-replace-refund-cancel
- **[documented-position]** The ACCC states that when a business sells a product that later develops a major problem, the business must give the consumer the choice of a remedy.

# unsolicited consumer agreements

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

The ACCC found that businesses are frequently breaching unsolicited consumer agreement safeguards and that non-compliance remains widespread. Unsolicited consumer agreements carry a cooling-off period; the ACCC states it is 10 business days after signing, while LegalVision states 10 days, so those accounts conflict. During the cooling-off period a seller must not supply goods over $500 or services or accept payment, but may supply products costing less than $500 including GST, and the period becomes six months if the salesperson does not tell the consumer about it. The consumer can terminate despite affirming the agreement or full execution; in a dispute the business must prove the agreement is not unsolicited; the current maximum penalties are $50,000 for a corporation and $10,000 for an individual; and the Schedule 4 amendment applies only to agreements entered into on or after the day that Schedule commences.

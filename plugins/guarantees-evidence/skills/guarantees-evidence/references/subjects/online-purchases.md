# online purchases

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

For online purchases made from 1 January 2011, the Australian Consumer Law generally applies as it does in physical shops: online businesses have the same responsibilities, all usual consumer rights apply, and an overseas business supplying goods or services directly to consumers in Australia must follow the law, but purchases are not covered if the business does not directly offer its products or services in Australia. There is a conflict about private sellers: Consumer Affairs Victoria states the Australian Consumer Law does not apply when a consumer buys from a private seller and the item is not sold in the course of the seller's business, while the ACCC states only some consumer guarantees do not apply; a buyer is still guaranteed clear title unless told otherwise before the sale. On marketplace platforms such as eBay, consumer guarantee rights apply to the seller the consumer buys from, not the platform, and return terms vary by marketplace and seller, for example the eBay Money Back Guarantee covers eBay.com.au purchases paid at eBay checkout for 30 days from estimated or actual delivery, BIG W and Kmart marketplace returns can only be processed online, and Bunnings Marketplace purchases must be directed to the third party seller. In practice it can be difficult to get a repair, replacement or refund from an overseas business when there is a problem.

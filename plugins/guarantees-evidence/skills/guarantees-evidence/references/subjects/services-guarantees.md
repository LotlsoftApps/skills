# services guarantees

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

The Australian Consumer Law gives consumers statutory guarantees for services, including that the work must be at least as good as a competent provider with average skills and experience and that services supplied for a disclosed purpose must be reasonably fit for that purpose; LegalVision describes these as warranties, but the ACCC states that warranties are voluntary and additional to consumer guarantees. For a failure that can be remedied and is not major, the consumer may require the supplier to fix it within a reasonable time; Consumer Affairs Victoria states this, while LegalVision states that the supplier chooses the remedy for a minor service fault. For a major or irremediable failure, the consumer may terminate the contract and recover compensation for any reduction in value below the price paid or payable; Consumer Affairs Victoria says cancelling a services contract gives a right to a refund, and Sprintlaw says the customer can cancel and get a refund for the unused portion and may claim compensation for loss or damage. A major failure includes a service creating an unsafe situation and, for contracts entered on or after the commencement of the 2020 amending Act, services that a reasonable consumer would not have acquired fully aware of the failures; services guarantees do not apply to insurance contracts, services bought before 1 January 2011 are not covered by the current Australian Consumer Law, and for purchases before 1 July 2021 the consumer-definition threshold amount is $40,000.

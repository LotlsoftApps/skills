# reasonable time

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

Statutory consumer guarantees last for a reasonable time after purchase, not for a fixed period; some salespeople in CHOICE's mystery shop told callers their rights were limited to a fixed period such as two years, which is not the legal position. For goods, a reasonable time depends on the type of product, the price paid, and how long a regular customer would expect the item to last; for services, it can depend on the nature of the services, weather, and availability of parts or materials, and the time a business has to fix a problem depends on the nature of the product or service, so an installed dishwasher repair at a house may take longer than a pair of pants repaired in-store. In Consumer Affairs Victoria's example, a consumer would not be entitled to a remedy where the sole reason a painting job took longer was weather outside the painter's control, but it is a major problem if the consumer would never have bought the service had they known how long it would take. The right to reject goods runs from the date of supply until the fault would reasonably be expected to appear; manufacturers and importers must provide spare parts and repair facilities for a reasonable time after purchase even if the consumer bought elsewhere; and products and services bought before 1 January 2011 are not covered by the current Australian Consumer Law, though they may be covered by older laws.

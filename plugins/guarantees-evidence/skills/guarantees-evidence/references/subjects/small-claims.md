# small claims

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

In Victoria, consumer disputes and small claims about products or services a person bought or sold are heard in VCAT's Civil Claims List; applicants generally must apply within six years of the dispute, and a claim to revoke the sale of a car bought for less than $40,000 must be made within three months of the purchase date. VCAT cannot help with cases heard under federal law instead of Victorian law, and in some cases where one party lives in another state or is a Commonwealth government organisation. The ACCC says each state has a small claims court or tribunal for consumer issues, but its guidance also says there is no relevant tribunal in Western Australia, where the Magistrates' Court can deal with claims not exceeding $75,000 including costs; in Victoria there is not a separate Small Claims Court. A VCAT payment order usually needs to be certified in a court before enforcement, many simple unpaid business-to-business invoices fall outside VCAT unless the dispute sits squarely in its consumer/trader or fair trading jurisdiction, and only the courts can make formal decisions on whether a breach of the law occurred.

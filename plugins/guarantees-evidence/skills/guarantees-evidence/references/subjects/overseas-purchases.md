# overseas purchases

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; the ACCC and the state regulators rewrite their guidance without announcing it, and this corpus found three of them publishing different maximum penalties at the same time.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

Selling parallel imports is an accepted method of sale in Australia, but a business selling them must comply with Australian product safety and labelling requirements and must not mislead consumers about their refund, repair or return rights. A consumer who buys a parallel import has all their usual rights, and the seller must offer a repair, replacement or refund if those rights are not met; the seller cannot refuse to help or tell the consumer to contact the manufacturer or local authorised sellers. A local manufacturer of the same product is not responsible for fixing a parallel-imported product or offering product support. Where the seller is overseas, the Australian Consumer Law applies to anyone conducting business within Australia, including overseas businesses, but a buyer may face practical and legal difficulties obtaining a refund, repair or replacement or enforcing rights because the seller may be outside Australian courts’ jurisdiction; for payment problems, a credit card user can contact their bank or card provider to organise a chargeback, a PayPal user can file a dispute through PayPal’s Resolution Centre within 180 days of paying, and an eBay user can report an issue to eBay’s Resolution Centre up to 45 days after the sale, while money sent by instant cash transfer or direct deposit can be very difficult to track once collected.

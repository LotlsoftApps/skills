# domestic violence provisions

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; renting law is amended and the regulator rewrites its guidance, both without announcing it.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

The Residential Tenancies Amendment Act 2018, which included family and domestic violence reforms, was passed in September 2018, and section 236 commenced on 29 March 2021. A Victorian renter with a safety notice or intervention order can change the locks without the rental provider or agent's permission; Consumer Affairs Victoria named sections 70A and 71 of the Residential Tenancies Act 1997 as the basis as of 26 March 2021, and section 70A was inserted by Act No. 52/2008. A renter experiencing family violence can apply to VCAT to change the rental agreement even if their name is not on it, or to leave before the agreement ends without paying for breaking the lease, and as of 7 December 2025 Consumer Affairs Victoria states VCAT must hear the application within 3 business days or no later than the next available day after those 3 business days. A renter's personal information must not be listed on a renter database if they breached the rental agreement because of an act of family or personal violence against them; the claims do not settle responsibility for property damage caused by family and domestic violence, as EBM RentCover states renters are responsible for that damage but also states a tenant can apply to VCAT to determine who pays.

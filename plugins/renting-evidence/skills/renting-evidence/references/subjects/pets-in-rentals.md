# pets in rentals

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; renting law is amended and the regulator rewrites its guidance, both without announcing it.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

Since Monday 2 March 2020, a Victorian renter must ask the rental provider for permission before keeping a pet, using Consumer Affairs Victoria's approved form; the new laws do not apply to a pet already in the rented home before that date. The rental provider must have a good reason to refuse and can refuse only by obtaining a VCAT order saying that refusal is reasonable; if the provider wants to refuse they must apply to VCAT within 14 days, and if they do not respond within 14 days consent is taken to have been given. A pet means any animal except an assistance dog, which is a dog trained to help a person with a disability, and a rental provider cannot charge a pet bond or give a notice to vacate for asking to have a pet. Rooming house residents can keep a pet only with the owner's permission, caravan park and residential park residents must follow their park rules, and a renter cannot be legally evicted just for having a pet when their agreement has a no pet clause, although VCAT has in some cases ordered removal.

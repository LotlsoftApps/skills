# fixed term agreement

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; renting law is amended and the regulator rewrites its guidance, both without announcing it.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

From 25 November 2025, a Victorian fixed-term agreement automatically becomes a periodic lease when it ends unless a new fixed-term agreement is signed or the rental provider gives a valid notice to vacate; certain notices to vacate have a minimum notice period of 90 days, and a rental provider cannot issue a notice to vacate at the end of a fixed term without a valid reason. During a fixed term, rent cannot be increased unless the agreement allows it and states how the increase will be calculated; for rooming house and caravan park agreements starting after 29 March 2021, rent cannot be increased more than once every 12 months. A renter who breaks a fixed-term agreement does not pay a penalty but may have to cover certain costs, the rental provider must try to re-let quickly, the renter does not pay rent after re-letting, a re-letting fee is limited to the same proportion of time left on the agreement, and a renter past the end of the first fixed-term lease cannot be charged advertising or re-letting fees to find a new renter. One point is unresolved: Landlord Wise says the one-month-per-year cap on lost rent applies to all standard-form agreements, while Consumer Affairs Victoria states it applies to fixed-term agreements longer than 5 years.

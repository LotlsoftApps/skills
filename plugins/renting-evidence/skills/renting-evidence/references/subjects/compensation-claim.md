# compensation claim

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; renting law is amended and the regulator rewrites its guidance, both without announcing it.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

Victoria has a compensation pathway through Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria, which launched on 23 June 2025 and as of 1 July 2026 deals with compensation claims including breach of duty. Tenants Victoria states that a renter must apply within 6 years, that failing to give written notice of the problem before applying may affect the claim, and that no notice of breach of duty is needed after moving out; it also advises renters to keep paying rent while seeking compensation. VCAT cannot award compensation for personal injury under the Residential Tenancies Act 1997, and it may order compensation for distress or disappointment only in exceptional circumstances where the parties could not reach agreement at Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria. The monetary limit is not settled: as of 1 March 2026 Tenants Victoria's rooming-house compensation page states $20,000, while its private-rental page and Justice Connect state $40,000, and Tenants Victoria states that a claim above the Victorian rental-law limit must instead be brought under the Australian Consumer Law and Fair Trading Act 2012.

### Practical effect (3)

- **[practical-effect]** Justice Connect's Homeless Law in Practice states that as a general rule the Tribunal will only award compensation for direct loss or damage, not for pain and suffering or physical injury.
  - as of: 2021-03-29
  - quote: "the Tribunal will only award compensation for direct loss or damage and not for pain and suffering or physical injury"
  - source: https://hlip.justiceconnect.org.au/practice-areas/housing-and-tenancy/during-a-tenancy/compensation/
- **[practical-effect]** The Victorian Council of Social Service reports that community sector workers describe renters as unable to assert their rights out of fear of 'rocking the boat'.
  - as of: 2026-05-29
  - quote: "Community sector workers report that a lack of affordable housing, a highly saturated rental market, and limited statutory enforcement of rental rights are stacking the deck against renters, perpetuating a power imbalance in which renters are unable to assert their rights out of fear of ‘rocking the boat’."
  - source: https://vcoss.org.au/housing-and-homelessness/2026/05/renting-in-victoria-2026/
- **[practical-effect]** Tenants Victoria observes that VCAT will not always decide cases where the compensation is still adding up.
  - as of: 2026-03-01
  - quote: "If your dispute ends up going to a formal VCAT hearing, VCAT will not always decide cases where the compensation is still adding up."
  - source: https://tenantsvic.org.au/explore-topics/issues-with-your-landlord/claiming-compensation/private-rental/

### Changed on (2)

- **[changed-on]** Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria helps settle disputes over bonds, compensation, repairs and excessive rents.
  - as of: 2025-06-23
  - quote: "Launched today, Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria (RDRV) is a dispute resolution service to help settle disputes over bonds, compensation, repairs, and excessive rents."
  - source: https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/latest-news/free-support-now-available-for-rental-disputes-in-victoria
- **[changed-on]** Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria was brought into law by the Consumer and Planning Legislation Amendment (Housing Statement Reform) Bill 2024.
  - as of: 2025-03-07
  - quote: "The legislation (called the Consumer and Planning Legislation Amendment (Housing Statement Reform) Bill 2024) also brings into law a new entity to improve the system for resolving issues in rental properties, called Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria (RDRV)."
  - source: https://tenantsvic.org.au/news/we-welcome-new-laws-for-renters/

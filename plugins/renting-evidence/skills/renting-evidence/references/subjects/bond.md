# bond

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; renting law is amended and the regulator rewrites its guidance, both without announcing it.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

In Victoria, a rental bond is held in trust by the Residential Tenancies Bond Authority; Victoria does not allow a separate pet bond, and in most cases a bond cannot be more than one month’s rent, while a rooming house bond cannot be more than 28 days’ rent for a resident on a fixed term rooming house agreement and 14 days’ rent for a resident who is not on a fixed term agreement, and a caravan park bond cannot be more than one month’s rent or hiring charge. A rental provider who receives the bond must lodge it, but the stated deadline conflicts: section 406 of the Residential Tenancies Act 1997, Tenants Victoria and several published sources say 10 business days, while Consumer Affairs Victoria’s bond page says 14 days not including public holidays. A rental provider must start a bond claim with the RTBA within 14 days of the rental agreement ending unless a renter initiates a claim first, and any renter named on the bond can start the claim themselves after the agreement ends without the rental provider’s agreement. From 13 October 2026 a rental provider must notify the renter in advance of a claim at the end of the agreement and provide supporting evidence; from 1 July 2026 a renter can move their bond to the next rental home under the Portable Rental Bond Scheme; the RTBA only repays the bond when everyone listed on the bond agrees or VCAT orders repayment, and rental providers cannot claim bond for fair wear and tear.

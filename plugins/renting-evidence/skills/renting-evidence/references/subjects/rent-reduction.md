# rent reduction

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; renting law is amended and the regulator rewrites its guidance, both without announcing it.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

In Victoria, a renter seeking a rent reduction must first ask Consumer Affairs Victoria for a free rent assessment, also called a rent increase investigation, within 30 days of receiving the written rent increase notice. If that report says a reduction should be given and the rental provider still refuses, the renter should apply to Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria within 30 days of receiving the report; RDRV applications are free, VCAT requires some rent increase disputes to go through RDRV first, renters cannot be forced to leave because they asked for a rent assessment, and a renter can apply to RDRV for a rent reduction if the landlord reduces or removes services or facilities and refuses to drop the rent. From 25 November 2025 the minimum rent increase notice period for rental providers and rooming house operators is 90 days, and the notice form must be given at least 90 days before the increase starts; some industry commentary still describes a 60-day minimum, but that conflicts with the 25 November 2025 change. The rent cannot be increased more than once every 12 months for rental agreements that started on or after 19 June 2019, or for rooming house, residential park and caravan park agreements starting after 29 March 2021, and the limit is once every 6 months for rental agreements that started before 19 June 2019; there is no statutory cap on the percentage increase, but an increase may be invalid if the rules are not followed, and a rental provider cannot ask a renter to pay more rent because the renter broke any part of the rental agreement.

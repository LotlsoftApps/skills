# rent arrears

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; renting law is amended and the regulator rewrites its guidance, both without announcing it.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

Consumer Affairs Victoria states that a renter under a Victorian residential rental agreement who is more than 14 days late on rent can be given a notice to vacate, and that the threshold is 7 days for a rooming house or caravan park tenant; Tenants Victoria describes unpaid rent of 14 days or more, which it says can be consecutive or accumulated. A renter can avoid eviction by paying all rent owing by the vacate date stated on the notice, and if all overdue rent is paid before that date VCAT must dismiss the landlord's eviction application; however, VCAT cannot dismiss a possession order application solely because the renter could pay off the unpaid rent under a payment plan, although if VCAT orders a payment plan it will put the application on hold, usually for 3 to 6 months. Rental providers do not have to be flexible about payment amounts or dates and can take formal action against a renter who is late paying rent, but they cannot charge an administration fee or late fee; a rental provider, rooming house operator, or park owner or operator cannot personally evict a renter, and only the police can evict with a VCAT warrant of possession, usually valid for 14 days; attempting to evict a tenant without following the correct legal process may result in significant penalties. A notice to vacate must be given by hand, post or electronic communication, and electronic communication requires the renter or resident's consent to receive notices that way; it cannot be sent by ordinary post, left in a letterbox or under a door, or handed to someone else.

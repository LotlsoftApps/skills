# modifications

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; renting law is amended and the regulator rewrites its guidance, both without announcing it.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

Since March 2021, Victorian renters can make certain changes without the rental provider’s consent if the property can be returned to its original state when they leave; Tenants Victoria states this is limited to changes that do not penetrate or permanently modify surfaces, fixtures or the structure. Without consent, renters may install non-permanent window film for insulation, reduced heat transfer or privacy, curtains while keeping the original curtains, a water-efficient shower head if the original is kept and the property is not heritage listed, picture hooks if the property is not heritage listed and exposed brick or concrete is not penetrated, and a removable, non-hard-wired security device that can be removed easily and does not affect neighbours’ privacy. Other changes need written consent, and providers cannot refuse without a good reason for painting, draughtproofing in a property without open flued gas heating, or disability-related modifications, and cannot unreasonably refuse reasonable security measures, modifications to increase thermal comfort or reduce energy and water costs, or a herb or vegetable garden; a VCAT application about unreasonably refused consent must be heard within 5 business days under section 64. A provider may require a modification bond to cover undoing changes, but not if the extra bond would be less than $500, and permission does not require the provider to pay; as of 2026-08-02, Consumer Affairs Victoria states that for a Victorian rental property with gas appliances a gas safety check must be less than six months old when draughtproofing work is completed.

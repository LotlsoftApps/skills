# utility charges

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; renting law is amended and the regulator rewrites its guidance, both without announcing it.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

In Victorian residential rentals, utility responsibility turns on whether the property has its own meter: where there is no separate meter, the rental provider pays the entire utility costs including electricity, gas and water; where the property has its own water meter, the renter must pay for the water they use and the sewage disposal charge unless the rental provider agrees to pay, and the rental provider remains responsible for charges relating to the sewerage infrastructure connecting to the house; where the property has its own electricity, gas or oil meter, the renter must pay all supply and usage costs unless the rental provider agrees to pay, and where bottled gas is used the renter pays for the supply and hire of gas bottles. The rental provider must pay to have the electricity, gas or oil supply installed and connected, and must pay the initial connection charges for electricity, water, gas including bottled gas, and phone and internet connections including the National Broadband Network, while renters must pay ongoing phone and internet usage costs unless the rental provider agrees otherwise; there is no minimum standard requiring a telephone line or internet connection, and a renter can negotiate an agreement for the rental provider to pay utility charges that is in writing and signed by the rental provider; an agreement about a solar energy system installed under the Victorian Solar Homes Program can make the renter liable for some installation costs and charges, unlike other initial connection costs. Rental providers must pay utility costs after the previous renter moves out until a new renter moves in and the utilities companies have the new renter's billing details, must reimburse a renter within 28 days of receiving a request to reimburse for utility costs that were the rental provider's responsibility, and must pay ongoing utility costs until a broken or faulty appliance is replaced with one that meets efficiency standards; new minimum energy efficiency standards for Victorian residential rental properties come into effect in phases from 1 March 2027, and an existing hot water system that permanently fails and cannot be repaired must be replaced with an energy-efficient electric hot water system. Some published advice conflicts on water: Soho Real Estate says the landlord takes care of the water and also that the renter must pay all charges including the water rates, while Consumer Affairs Victoria states the renter must pay for water they use and sewage disposal where the property has its own meter.

# vcat application

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; renting law is amended and the regulator rewrites its guidance, both without announcing it.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

In Victoria, anyone renting or managing a rental property can apply to Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria for disagreements about bonds, compensation, rent increases and repairs; participation is voluntary, an RDRV application is also a VCAT application, and a formal VCAT hearing can be requested at any point, but anything said or done at RDRV cannot be used as evidence at VCAT unless everyone agrees in writing. Disputes not dealt with by RDRV, such as evictions and ending a lease, go straight to VCAT for a formal hearing, but VCAT cannot help with disputes between neighbours, co-renters or rental providers, bond refunds with no dispute, or matters involving a party in another state, a Commonwealth government organisation or federal law. For a VCAT hearing, evidence must be given to other parties and submitted at least 3 days before the hearing, and hearing and appropriate dispute resolution fees must be paid at least 5 business days before the first hearing day; VCAT may dismiss or delay an application if claim details are not provided, and renter compensation claims are capped at $40,000 and must be applied for within 6 years of the problem or loss occurring. There is a conflict over RDRV's status: Tenants Victoria recommended an independent alternative dispute resolution scheme, while RDRV describes itself as a service provided by VCAT; from 31 March 2026, Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria, Consumer Affairs Victoria and VCAT can consider additional factors when determining if a proposed rent increase is excessive, with Consumer Affairs Victoria and VCAT taking CPI into account.

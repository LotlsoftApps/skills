# rent increase

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; renting law is amended and the regulator rewrites its guidance, both without announcing it.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

Consumer Affairs Victoria states that a rental provider cannot increase rent during a fixed term agreement unless the rental agreement has an additional clause allowing an increase; rent cannot be increased more than once every 12 months for agreements that started on or after 19 June 2019, and for agreements that started before 19 June 2019 the interval is 6 months. From 29 March 2021 a new fixed-term lease with a rent increase clause must state the amount of the increase or the method used to calculate it and that the rent will not be increased by more than that amount; a rent increase notice is invalid if the wrong form is used, an invalid notice has no effect, and Tenants Victoria states only one increase can be listed in each notice. The minimum notice period changed from 60 to 90 days on 25 November 2025 according to Consumer Affairs Victoria, but Collings Real Estate described 60 days as the minimum as of 26 June 2026. There is no statutory cap on the percentage by which rent can be increased; from 31 March 2026 Consumer Affairs Victoria and VCAT take the CPI into account when assessing whether an increase is excessive, and from the same date the Director of Consumer Affairs Victoria, Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria and VCAT can consider additional factors; Tenants Victoria states an increase above the relevant CPI is not necessarily excessive, and a renter can seek a free rent assessment from Consumer Affairs Victoria within 30 days of receiving the notice, and after 30 days the renter can no longer request one.

# smoke alarms

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; renting law is amended and the regulator rewrites its guidance, both without announcing it.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

Smoke alarms are compulsory in all Victorian residential properties including houses, units, flats and townhouses, must meet Australian Standard AS 3786, and buildings constructed after 1 August 1997 must have hard-wired smoke alarms with battery back-up, while rooming houses must have hard-wired smoke alarms. From 25 November 2025, rental providers have been required to arrange annual smoke alarm safety checks and to ensure smoke alarms are tested at least once every 12 months in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions, no matter when the rental agreement started. A smoke alarm that is not working is an urgent repair, and Tenants Victoria states a renter may request urgent repairs if a smoke alarm is not installed, not working correctly, or has not been tested in more than 12 months; if the rental provider cannot be contacted or does not immediately fix it, the renter can authorise and pay for an urgent repair of up to $2,500, and the provider must repay them within 7 days. Renters and rooming house residents must not deactivate, remove or interfere with a smoke alarm, and Consumer Affairs Victoria states the rental provider or rooming house operator may give a Notice of breach of duty, while one industry source states a notice to vacate can be given.

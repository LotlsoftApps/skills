# break lease

Compiled 2026-08-16. Every claim carries the date its quote was true; renting law is amended and the regulator rewrites its guidance, both without announcing it.

## Summary

Written from the claims below and checked against them.

In Victoria, a renter who ends a fixed-term lease early does not pay a penalty, but must cover certain costs so the rental provider does not lose money. The rental provider must try to re-let the property or room quickly and keep their losses to a minimum, and the renter does not pay rent after the property is re-let. Reasonable costs are not defined in law, and sources conflict on advertising: Consumer Affairs Victoria and Landlord Wise state that reasonable advertising paid to find a replacement renter may be recoverable, while Tenants Victoria states that lease-break costs are not the costs to find or advertise to a new renter and that a re-letting fee must be based on the fee for preparing the outgoing renter's lease. VCAT decides disputed reasonable costs, considers notice and re-letting efforts, may award little or no compensation in family violence, and cannot order more than one month's rent for every 12 months remaining on a fixed-term agreement longer than 5 years; a renter past the end of their first fixed-term lease cannot be charged advertising or a re-letting fee to find a new renter, and a renter experiencing severe hardship can apply to VCAT to break an agreement without paying any costs.
